About

Twelve years of Android, and the systems the apps plug into.

I started my career by building a company rather than joining one. In 2012, right after my CSE degree, I founded Power GP BD and led it for six years. We shipped more than 100 Android applications across enterprise, B2B and consumer verticals, and earned two awards from the Facebook Startup Program along the way. One of our apps, a multimedia product called Theatre, crossed 500,000 downloads on the Play Store (it is no longer listed). Those years taught me how to own an outcome completely.

After that I wanted to go deeper on the engineering itself, and the problems grew past the app. At Kutumbita I designed and built Kinship.ai on my own. It was my first complete system rather than just an Android client: real-time messaging over Socket.io, plus offline-first storage and background sync for factory workers who barely had a network. I took it from architecture through Play Store release. At Samsung R&D, as Lead Software Engineer II, the system spanned devices. I built health-tracking apps in the Samsung Health ecosystem and wearable companions that sync losslessly over BLE to watches running Tizen, with server-side sync behind them. I also shipped features in the SmartThings iOS app.

In 2022 I joined Plentina, a US fintech bringing Buy-Now-Pay-Later to the Philippines and Vietnam. I led the Android side, where a failed payment costs real money. That meant payment gateway integrations, Kotlin Flow for state, and a full migration to Jetpack Compose while Compose was still in beta.

At Avetics Global I owned the ground-up rewrite of two production Android apps that control live aircraft. The stack is multi-module Compose with Hilt, a real-time telemetry layer over custom TCP sockets, a vendor video pipeline, and computer vision that lets a drone land on a QR code. I also set the architecture standards for the six-person Android team I mentored. The domain is drones, but the engineering is what any connected product needs.

Looking back, each role handed me a harder connected-system problem: devices, protocols, video, hardware. Android is still where I go deepest, and these days the systems around the app are a big part of the job.

Alongside the day job I teach. Google selected me as a mentor for its Exchange with Google Developers pilot, and I have trained 100+ developers across Asia, plus university students and corporate teams. More on the mentoring work.
