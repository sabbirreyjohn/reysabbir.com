Partner with me · Equity CTO & technical co-founder

Your CTO — before you can afford one.

You’re a non-technical founder with a real idea. Investors want to see a product. Building a product takes engineers. Engineers cost money you’ll have after the raise. Every early founder knows this loop — most get stuck in it.

Here’s my answer: I become your CTO under contract, now. I build your app and your entire technical side while you raise — and I’m compensated with a percentage plus company ownership when the funding lands.

Call it a fractional CTO, an equity CTO, or the technical co-founder you haven’t found yet. The label matters less than the arrangement: you get a senior engineer accountable for the entire technical side of your company, at a stage when hiring one is impossible.

The engagement

What you get

Your MVP, built end-to-end

I’m a dedicated MVP builder. From first wireframe to Play Store release, the product gets designed, architected and shipped — by one senior engineer who has done it for 12+ years.

Every technical decision, handled

Stack choices, architecture, vendors, releases — the entire technical side of your company is my problem, not yours. You get a CTO, not a contractor.

Weekly progress meetings

A standing weekly check-in, in plain English. You always know exactly where the product stands and what ships next.

You focus on funding & marketing

The division of labour is explicit: you own fundraising, marketing and customers. I own everything technical. That’s the whole point of the model.

Investor & pitch support

Technical due-diligence answers, architecture slides, roadmap credibility — when investors ask hard questions, your CTO is in the room.

Your future team, hired right

When funding lands, I interview and hire your first engineers and hand over a codebase they’ll thank you for. I’ve trained 100+ developers — building teams is familiar ground.

Compensation

Flexible by design: pay as we build, or a percentage plus company ownership when you raise. We agree the structure before a single line of code is written.

Fit matters

I don’t say yes to every idea

I take on a small number of partnerships at a time — the model only works when both sides commit. Here’s what I look for:

01

You’re committed

This model only works with founders who are all-in on their venture. If it’s a side experiment, I’m not the right partner.

02

You own the business side

I take the technical hassle completely off your plate — which means you must be ready to drive fundraising and marketing with the same intensity.

03

We put it in writing

Scope, percentage and equity are agreed up front in a clear contract. Clean terms protect both of us and make your cap table investor-ready.
