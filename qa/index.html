Q&A

Questions I get asked a lot.

Most first conversations cover the same ground, so here are my answers in my own words.

On V-Assist, I owned a premium feature the company had already marketed. The drone scans a QR code from the air, tracks it in flight, and lands precisely on top of it, from the Android-based DJI remote control.

Close to release, it was failing. At high image quality, QR scanning lagged behind the live feed, so the drone couldn’t follow the code in time to land. The easy fix was to drop the image quality and ship. Instead I debugged the pipeline line by line, and the real bottleneck turned out to be the video codec and rendering path. Detection was fine.

Working from the codec documentation, I rebalanced decoding and rendering so the feed kept its quality while scanning ran in real time. The feature shipped on schedule, the drone lands on the mark without crashing, and it’s live in production today. I try to work that way in general: find the actual cause before trading away part of the product.

The one closest to my heart is Theatre, a multimedia app from my Power GP BD years that crossed 500,000 downloads on the Play Store (it’s no longer live). Half a million people chose something I built, and I still think about that.

The most recent one is Football Predictor 2026. FIFA’s new 48-team format is algorithmically nasty: the Round of 32 depends on third-place results across all 12 groups, with 450+ valid bracket combinations. I built an engine that resolves the whole knockout tree in real time, by the letter of the FIFA regulations. It turned something that confuses everyone into something anyone can play with.

I translate. I can sit with product managers, designers and stakeholders, work out what they actually need, and turn it into technical decisions. After twelve years of shipping end to end, I do more than write the code: I design the architecture, build it, release it and keep it running.

I also automate repetitive work instead of putting up with it, and I stay calm when a deadline gets tight. I would rather find the smart fix than pull an all-nighter. Ask my last three teams about release week.

I make the decisions and the agents do the typing. Architecture, tech stack and library choices are mine. The agents speed up the implementation, but I keep track of what they use, steer them off the wrong library and onto the right one, and shape the code so it holds up later with fewer bugs.

I keep up with the tooling so I can direct it well. Every line that ships is one I have reviewed and can defend.

Android is my deepest stack, and the work I enjoy most puts it inside a larger system: hardware, real-time data, sync across devices, payments. That is where architecture decisions really matter.

The common thread is ownership. I do my best work when I am accountable for the outcome, not just the ticket, and when I can build something I am willing to stand behind.
